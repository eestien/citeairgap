why not a general-purpose assistant

General AI assistants are excellent for drafting and research. Regulated answers need something different: proof, and data that never leaves.

general_ai_assistant great tool · different job
  • Strong at writing, summarising and reasoning across broad topics
  • Citations are usually at document or page level, so a reviewer still has to find the exact wording
  • Usually cloud-hosted. Documents classified Confidential or above often can't be sent to it
  • Built to be helpful, so it may fill a gap with general knowledge where your policy is silent
  • Usage logs live with the vendor, not in your own tamper-evident audit trail
citeairgap built for compliance answers
  • Answers only from your corpus. Every claim must match the source text, or it is removed
  • Cites the exact clause, character range and file hash, so a reviewer can check it in seconds
  • Model runs on your own hardware, with no outbound connections
  • Says "not found" when your documents don't answer the question
  • Hash-chained audit log stays in-house and exports to your SIEM
how it works

The AI drafts. Deterministic checks decide what you see.

A local language model writes a draft. It never gets the final say. Each claim has to carry a verbatim quote that the system can find in your documents. Any claim that fails is removed before the answer reaches you, and the removal is logged.

faq

Questions we hear from security, legal and IT.

Does any data leave our network?

No. The language model, the search index, the documents and the logs all stay on your hardware. The application blocks outbound connections, and in production that is backed by host firewall rules and a network with no route to the internet.

Can it still hallucinate?

The model can still draft an incorrect claim. What changes is that the claim won't reach you unsupported. Each one has to quote your documents and pass span, number and meaning checks. Claims that fail are removed and logged, and you can see what was rejected and why.

What kinds of documents does it work with?

Policies, procedures, contracts, standards, regulations and internal guidelines: any controlled text your people need answers from. We scope formats, languages and volume with you during the pilot.

What hardware do we need?

A pilot runs on a single modern laptop or workstation. Department deployments usually use an on-premise server with a GPU, sized to the number of users and documents.

How do auditors check an answer?

Every citation includes the document ID, clause, character offsets and a SHA-256 hash of the source file, so anyone can open the original and confirm the exact text. The audit log is hash-chained and can be exported to your SIEM.

Does it replace our experts?

No. It gives them the right clause in seconds, with proof, so decisions stay with people and stay traceable.
